AMENDMENT 1

Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1951

The First Amendment was the first constitutional amendment in independent India. It was enacted to address the challenges related to freedom of speech, land reforms, reservations and other socio-economic issues.
KEY HIGHLIGHTS
  • Introduced reasonable restrictions on Freedom of Speech under Article 19(2).
  • Empowered the State to make laws for land reforms and imposed restrictions on property rights under Article 31A and 31B.
  • Added the Ninth Schedule to protect certain laws from judicial review.
  • Provided for reservation in educational institutions for socially and educationally backward classes under Article 15(4).
  • Changed the term “estate” to “estates” in Article 31.
CHANGES INTRODUCED BY THE FIRST AMENDMENT
Article Change Made Details
Article 15(4) New Clause added State can make special provisions for the advancement of any socially and educationally backward classes or for SCs and STs in educational institutions.
Article 19(2) Clause modified Freedom of speech can be restricted on grounds of Public Order, Friendly Relations with Foreign States, Incitement to an offence, etc.
Article 31A New Article added Protected laws providing for acquisition of estates, interests in estates, and extinguishment or modification of rights in such estates.
Article 31B New Article added Protected laws included in the Ninth Schedule from being challenged in any court.
Ninth Schedule New Schedule added A list of laws shielded from judicial review under Article 31B.
Article 31 Word amended The word “estate” replaced by “estates”.
BACKGROUND

After the Supreme Court’s judgments in Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras (1950) and Brij Bhushan v. State of Delhi (1950), the government felt that freedom of speech was being interpreted too broadly. Also, land reform laws were being struck down. Therefore, the First Amendment was brought to remove these difficulties.

SIGNIFICANCE
  • Balanced Fundamental Rights with societal interests.
  • Strengthened the power of the State to implement land reforms.
  • Laid the foundation for future social justice measures.
  • One of the most significant amendments in India’s constitutional history.
EXAM NOTE
The First Amendment is often called the “Most Important Amendment” because it introduced reasonable restrictions on fundamental rights and protected land reform laws which were crucial for the newly independent nation.

WHAT DOES IT DO?

It ensures that the State can make laws for land reforms, provides special provisions for backward classes and allows reasonable restrictions on freedom of speech in the interest of public order, security and other grounds.
