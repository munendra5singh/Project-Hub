ARTICLE 1

Name and Territory of the Union

1. ORIGINAL ARTICLE TEXT
  1. India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.
  2. The States and the territories thereof shall be as specified in the First Schedule.
  3. The territory of India shall comprise—
    • (a) the territories of the States;
    • (b) the Union territories specified in the First Schedule; and
    • (c) such other territories as may be acquired.
2. SIMPLE EXPLANATION
  1. India (which is also known as Bharat) is a Union of States.
  2. All States and Union Territories of India are specified in the First Schedule.
  3. The territory of India comprises:
    • (a) Territories of the States
    • (b) Territories of the Union Territories specified in the First Schedule
    • (c) Any other territories that may be acquired by India in the future
3. WHAT DOES ARTICLE 1 SAY?

Article 1 declares India as a "Union of States" and defines the constituent parts that form the territory of India. This Article lays the foundation of India's political identity and geographical structure.

4. WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF ARTICLE 1?

The purpose of this Article is to establish that India is an indivisible Union and to define the scope of its territory. It serves as the baseline for subsequent provisions (Articles 2, 3, and 4).

5. MEANING OF "INDIA, THAT IS BHARAT"

In the Constitution, the country's name is specified as "India, that is Bharat." This means that Bharat is the official indigenous name while India is its international name. Both names hold equal official validity.

6. MEANING OF "UNION OF STATES"

India is defined as a "Union of States" rather than a "Federation". This signifies that the Indian Union is indestructible. Reorganization, alteration of boundaries, or creation of new states fall under the jurisdiction of Parliament. No state has the right to secede from the Union.

7. WHAT DOES THE TERRITORY OF INDIA INCLUDE?
  • Territories of the States – The geographical areas of all individual States.
  • Union Territories – Centrally administered territories listed in the First Schedule.
  • Other Territories – Any territories that may be acquired by India in the future.
8. RELATION WITH THE FIRST SCHEDULE

Article 1(2) specifies that the list of States and Union Territories is contained in the First Schedule. This Schedule can be amended over time through parliamentary law (under Articles 2 and 3).

EXAM NOTE / KEY TAKEAWAY
Article 1 is the cornerstone of India's identity and territorial jurisdiction. Key memory hooks: India – Bharat, Union of States, Territory defined in First Schedule.
KEY POINTS
  • India, that is Bharat, is a Union of States.
  • States and Union Territories are listed in the First Schedule.
  • Territory of India includes States, Union Territories, and acquired territories.
  • India is an indestructible Union.
  • Parliament has the power to create new States or Union Territories and alter areas, boundaries, or names.
IMPORTANT TERMS

India/Bharat: The official name of the country is Bharat, with India being its recognized international designation.

Union: An indestructible political association of States under a central governance framework.

State: A constitutional territorial unit having its own elected government.

Union Territory: An administrative division governed directly by the President through an appointed Administrator.

Territory of India: The entire geographical expanse over which sovereign Indian jurisdiction extends.

EXAMPLES
  • States: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, etc.
  • Union Territories: Delhi, Puducherry, Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, etc.
  • Acquired Territories: For example, Goa was acquired by India in 1961.
CONSTITUTION FACTS
  • The Constitution of India came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • Originally, the Constitution contained 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules.
  • Dr. B. R. Ambedkar is known as the Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution.
  • The Constitution is the supreme law of India and every law must conform to it.
