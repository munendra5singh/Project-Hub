ARTICLE 2

Admission or Establishment of New States

1. ORIGINAL ARTICLE TEXT

Parliament may by law admit into the Union, or establish, new States on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit.

2. SIMPLE EXPLANATION

Article 2 gives Parliament the power to bring foreign territories or non-existing territories into the Union of India as new States, based on terms and conditions decided by Parliament.

3. WHAT DOES ARTICLE 2 SAY?

Article 2 grants exclusive legislative power to the Parliament of India to expand the Union. It allows the integration of foreign territories that were not previously a part of India, either by admitting them directly as States or by establishing new States out of acquired territories.

4. WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF ARTICLE 2?

The primary purpose of Article 2 is to facilitate the peaceful integration and constitutional recognition of external territories acquired by India through treaty, purchase, conquest, or agreement into the sovereign framework of the Union of India.

5. TWO POWERS GRANTED TO PARLIAMENT
  • Power to Admit: Refers to the admission of states that are already in existence as political entities outside the Union (e.g., admitting an independent territory).
  • Power to Establish: Refers to the creation and establishment of new states out of acquired territories that were not previously organized as states.
6. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN ARTICLE 2 AND ARTICLE 3

While both articles deal with states, their scopes are distinctly different:

  • Article 2: Deals with the admission or establishment of new states that were not previously part of India (external territorial expansion).
  • Article 3: Deals with the internal reorganization of existing states within India (altering names, boundaries, or creating new states from existing ones).
EXAM NOTE / KEY TAKEAWAY
Article 2 relates ONLY to territories that were NOT part of India before. For internal restructuring of existing Indian states, Article 3 is applied.
KEY POINTS
  • Empowers Parliament to admit or establish new States.
  • Applies exclusively to non-existing or external territories.
  • Parliament determines the terms and conditions of admission.
  • Distinct from Article 3 which handles internal reorganization.
IMPORTANT TERMS

Admission: Accepting an already existing political unit or state into the Union.

Establishment: Forming a new state out of acquired territory not organized previously.

External Territory: Land or region lying outside the current constitutional borders of India.

EXAMPLES
  • Sikkim (1974–1975): Initially introduced as an associate state under Article 2A (35th Amendment) and later admitted as a full state (36th Amendment).
  • Puducherry & Goa: Foreign territories acquired and subsequently integrated into the legal framework of India.
