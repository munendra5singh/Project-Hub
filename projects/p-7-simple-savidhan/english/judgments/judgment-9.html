SUPREME COURT OF INDIA

State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)

This was one of the earliest and most important judgments of the Supreme Court of India which upheld the supremacy of Fundamental Rights over Directive Principles of State Policy.
CASE OVERVIEW
Court : Supreme Court of India
Date of Judgment : 29 April 1951
Bench : Chief Justice M. Patanjali Sastri, Justice Fazl Ali, Justice Kania, Justice M. L. Sethi, Justice B. K. Mukherjee
Case Type : Constitutional Law – Fundamental Rights
Citation : AIR 1951 SC 226
BACKGROUND OF THE CASE

The Government of Madras issued a Communal G.O. (Government Order) providing for reservation of seats in educational institutions on the basis of communal representation (Muslims, Christians, Backward Classes, etc.).

Champakam Dorairajan, who belonged to a non-reserved category, challenged this order as it denied her admission to a medical college despite her higher marks.

FACTS OF THE CASE

The State of Madras had prescribed a system of communal reservation in educational institutions. The petitioner, Champakam Dorairajan, argued that the order violated her Fundamental Right to equality under Article 29(2) and Article 15(1) of the Constitution.

ISSUES BEFORE THE COURT
  • 1 Whether the Communal G.O. violated Article 29(2) of the Constitution?
  • 2 Whether Article 15(1) prohibits the State from making any discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth?
JUDGMENT
The Supreme Court held that the Communal G.O. was violative of Article 29(2) and Article 15(1) of the Constitution and therefore, it was unconstitutional and void.
REASONING OF THE COURT
  • Article 29(2) guarantees that no citizen shall be denied admission in any educational institution maintained by the State or receiving aid out of State funds on grounds only of religion, race, caste, language or any of them.
  • Article 15(1) prohibits the State from discriminating against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth.
  • Directive Principles (like Article 46) cannot override Fundamental Rights.
  • Fundamental Rights are justiciable and prevail over Directive Principles in case of conflict.
SIGNIFICANCE OF THE JUDGMENT
  • Upheld the supremacy of Fundamental Rights.
  • Established that Directive Principles cannot override Fundamental Rights.
  • Led to the First Constitutional Amendment, 1951, which inserted Article 15(4) and Article 31A.

WHAT DOES IT MEAN?

This judgment clearly laid down that Fundamental Rights are the supreme law of the land. The State cannot make any law or order that takes away or infringes these rights.

It was a turning point in India's constitutional journey.

IMPACT OF THE JUDGMENT
  • Strengthened the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
  • Triggered the First Amendment Act, 1951.
  • Inserted Article 15(4) to allow special provisions for socially and educationally backward classes.
  • Inserted Article 31A and 31B to protect certain laws from judicial review.
