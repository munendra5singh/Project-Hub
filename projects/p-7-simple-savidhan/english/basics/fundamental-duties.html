ARTICLE 51A

Fundamental Duties of Citizens

1. ARTICLE 51A - 11 FUNDAMENTAL DUTIES

It shall be the duty of every citizen of India—

  • (a) To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem;
  • (b) To cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle for freedom;
  • (c) To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India;
  • (d) To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so;
  • (e) To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India transcending religious, linguistic and regional or sectional diversities; to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women;
  • (f) To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture;
  • (g) To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wild life, and to have compassion for living creatures;
  • (h) To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform;
  • (i) To safeguard public property and to abjure violence;
  • (j) To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement;
  • (k) To provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years (Added by 86th Amendment, 2002).
2. SIMPLE EXPLANATION

Just as the Constitution grants us Fundamental Rights, it also expects civic and moral responsibilities from its citizens in the form of Fundamental Duties. These 11 duties serve as a constant reminder that while enjoying our rights, we must also perform our obligations towards society, the environment, and the nation.

3. HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
  • The original Constitution of India (1950) did not contain Fundamental Duties.
  • They were incorporated into Part IV-A through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976, upon the recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee.
  • Initially, 10 Fundamental Duties were added to Article 51A.
  • The 11th Fundamental Duty (duty regarding education for children) was added by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002.
4. IMPORTANCE & ENFORCEABILITY

Fundamental Duties are non-justiciable in nature, meaning they cannot be directly enforced by courts nor is there any direct constitutional punishment for their violation. However, Parliament has enacted several statutory laws (e.g., Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, Wildlife Protection Act) to enforce specific duties effectively.

EXAM NOTE / KEY TAKEAWAY
Remember: Part IV-A | Article 51A | Total 11 Duties | Borrowed from USSR Constitution | Added by 42nd Amendment (1976) & 86th Amendment (2002).
KEY POINTS
  • Applies only to Citizens of India (not foreigners).
  • Non-justiciable in nature.
  • Inspired by USSR Constitution.
  • Referred to Swaran Singh Committee.
IMPORTANT TERMS

Part IV-A: Separate Part dedicated to Fundamental Duties.

Article 51A: The single Article containing all 11 duties.

Scientific Temper: Duty to foster logic, inquiry, and scientific thinking.
