Culture, Traditions and Moral Values (CTMV)

This textbook covers Culture vs Civilization (MacIver definition), attributes of Indian heritage (Unity in Diversity), universal ethical values (Satya, Ahimsa, Karuna, Dharma), Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam, Nishkama Karma, constitutional values (Preamble & Article 51A(g)), and Digital Ethics.

Unit I

Indian Cultural Heritage

1.1 Culture vs. Civilization

E.B. Tylor's Definition of Culture

"Culture is that complex whole which includes knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom, and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society."

Parameter Culture Civilization
MacIver Definition "What we are" (Internal mental, moral, and artistic values). "What we use" (External physical apparatus, technology, and tools).
Measurement Intangible; difficult to quantify numerically. Tangible; easily measured via technological output.
Progress Does not advance linearly. Advances unidirectionally with technological innovation.
Unit II

Traditions and Universal Moral Values

2.1 Universal Ethical Values: Satya, Ahimsa, Karuna & Dharma

Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam & Nishkama Karma

1. Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam (Maha Upanishad): "The World is One Family" — promoting universal fraternity beyond narrow parochial boundaries.

2. Nishkama Karma (Bhagavad Gita II.47): Performing duty selflessly without attachment to fruits or outcomes.

Unit III

Constitutional Ethics and Environmental Duties

3.1 Preamble Values & Article 51A(g) Fundamental Duty

Article 51A(g) Mandate

"It shall be the duty of every citizen of India to protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures."

Practice MCQs
Q1. According to MacIver, Culture represents which of the following?
A) What we use
B) What we are
C) Our physical tools
D) Machines and roads
Explanation: MacIver defined Culture as "What we are" and Civilization as "What we use".
